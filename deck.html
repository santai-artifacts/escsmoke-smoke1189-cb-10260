Espresso Chronicles

1

The History of Espresso

From Italian invention to global obsession

Open by asking the audience how many cups of espresso they've had today. Espresso is one of the most consumed beverages in the world — and its story starts in late-19th-century Italy.
2

The Problem: Coffee Was Too Slow

  • By the 1880s, coffee bars in Italy struggled with long brew times
  • A single cup could take **4–5 minutes** to prepare
  • Factory workers had short breaks — demand for speed was intense
  • Entrepreneurs began experimenting with steam pressure as a shortcut
Frame this as a labor story, not just a beverage story. The pressure (literal and figurative) of industrializing Italy drove the invention. "Espresso" means "pressed out" or "fast" in Italian — the word itself captures the urgency.
3

1901: Angelo Moriondo & Luigi Bezzera

  • **Angelo Moriondo** patented the first espresso machine in Turin, 1884
  • **Luigi Bezzera** refined it dramatically in 1901 — per-cup brewing, less bitterness
  • Desiderio Pavoni bought Bezzera's patent and commercialized the machine
  • The "La Pavoni" debuted publicly at the 1906 Milan Fair
Moriondo is often overlooked — his machine was a bulk brewer for his hotel, not a per-cup device. Bezzera's key insight was individual-cup extraction, which is the model we still use today. Pavoni was the businessman who scaled it.
4

Mid-Century: The Modern Espresso Emerges

  • **1938:** Cremonesi & Bezzera introduce piston-based machines — no more steam bitterness
  • **1948:** Achille Gaggia patents the spring-lever piston, creating modern espresso
  • Gaggia's 9-bar pressure produces **crema** — the golden foam we now expect
  • Italian café culture explodes; espresso becomes central to daily life
This is the pivotal era. Steam machines over-extracted and scorched the coffee. Gaggia's lever machine hit ~9 bars of pressure without steam, which is the sweet spot that creates crema and smooth flavor. Crema was initially called "caffè crema" and marketed as a sign of quality.
5

Espresso Goes Global

  • 1950s–60s: Italian immigrants bring espresso culture to North America and Australia
  • **1971:** Starbucks opens in Seattle — initially a coffee bean retailer
  • **1982:** Howard Schultz visits Milan, is inspired by espresso bars
  • Third-wave coffee movement (2000s–present): single-origin, precision extraction
The globalization of espresso is really a story of Italian diaspora first, corporate chains second, and artisan revival third. Today, specialty coffee shops obsess over extraction time (25–30 seconds), grind size, and water temperature to a degree Bezzera never imagined. Close by noting that each cup contains about 150 years of engineering.
